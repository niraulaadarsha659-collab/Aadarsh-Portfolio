Words I Never Said Aloud

Volume II — Poems from the War I Never Chose

Free to Read
Act I

Before the Storm

I used to think mornings were guaranteed.

The Boy Who Thought Life Was Easy

There once lived a boy inside me,
Who believed the world was always benevolent.
He chased tomorrow with open hands,
Never fearing what it might generate.

He wore the morning like a crown,
As though the sun had risen just for him.
Every road stretched wide before his feet,
And every horizon welcomed him in.

Good grades slipped in like passing clouds,
Without demanding any sleepless nights.
Friendships bloomed with open arms,
And success felt ordinary.

Doors opened before I learned to knock.
Fortune often reached me first.
He mistook blessings as his own strength,
And calm waters for a steady world.

He had never met a storm,
So believed the sea was always still.
He had never carried sorrow,
So assumed every heart was light.

No one had taught him,
That victories are conquered,
Or that life strikes with sudden shock,
When we least expect the knock.

If something felt unfair,
He stood against it without hesitation.
Not because he was fearless,
But because he had never tasted consequences.

He believed justice always found its way.
He believed kindness always returned.
He believed tomorrow,
Would look just like today.
The way he trusted every sunrise,
Never questioning the price.
The way he held the world so dear,
Never dreaming it would disappear.

He didn't know,
An ordinary day would divide his life
Into a before...
And an after.

He didn't know
That some wars
Never asks permission,
Just appears out of nowhere.

He simply smiled,
Stepped into tomorrow,
And met the life
That was ready to break everything he held dear.

The Dreams I Forgot

A time full of daydreams.
A stethoscope in one hand,
And syringes in the other.

School felt easier than before;
Ambition felt close enough to touch.
The future was never a question;
I always wanted to heal the broken.

The dream never changed its shape.
The certainty never did.

Then, without knocking,
illness let itself in.
Classrooms turned into waiting rooms.
Lectures turned into lab reports.
The seat that once held me
Learned to sit empty.

I kept telling myself,
It was only for now.
But now became weeks,
And weeks became a version of me:
I didn't recognize.

I stopped measuring my life in exams.
I started measuring it in appointments,
In good days,
In how long I could last
Before the tiredness won.

The mirror stopped asking who I'd become.
It started asking where I'd gone.
I had no answer,
Only the quiet habit of comparing,
Who I was?
To who I am supposed to be.

If I could reach that boy now,
I would grab his hand and tell him:
This pain is just a visitor,
not a permanent resident.

The War I Never Chose

When every morning began with a smile,
When elation filled my every mile.
The wind felt warm, the sky was bright,
And optimism would gently kiss me each night.

Then quietly, without a sound,
A storm arrived and held its ground.
Not one of thunder, rain, or flame,
But one that hushed my name.

Each day became another fight,
A battle hidden from plain sight.
My body weakened, spirit frayed,
While countless fears filled up my head.

I fought through days, months and years,
With silent prayers and unseen tears.
I broke like waves still rose like tides,
Conquering the fear that hides.

There came a night so cold, so deep,
Where even dreams refused to sleep.
I was ready to greet death,
Just to release this painful breath.

Not because I wanted to die,
But I had simply asked life, "Why?"
When every breath had become a chore,
I wasn't sure I had much more.

Act II

Becoming a Battlefield

The ICU didn't just break my body—it taught me how quiet fear can be.

The Body That Betrayed Me

It didn't happen like a sudden crash,
But happened in small thefts.
A step that once was easy,
Has now started asking permission.

I kept waiting for my body,
To return to yesterday.
But tomorrow only borrowed more,
And left me suffering in return.

Stairs became mountains.
Standing still became a task.
I learned to calculate a room,
Before I dared to cross it.

The mornings arrived not with light,
But with sudden chills and hollow aches.
The weight of my own bones grew heavy,
Like a physical anchor dragging me down.

The mirror stayed the same shape,
But I no longer recognize,
What stood inside it?
A stranger wearing my name..

Now I carry myself,
The way you carry
Something fragile,
Something borrowed.

The independence I took for granted,
Evaporated into a quiet shame.
I used to carry my own world.
Now, I am the heavy weight that someone else must carry.

The Weight Nobody Sees

They look and see a quiet room,
With a body resting in the afternoon light.
They offer gentle words, a casual phrase,
"Get well soon," wrapped in simple hope.

I smiled back through a lie.
Faking a peace,
I do not possess,
Just to comfort their heavy hearts.

They saw the medicines,
The missed days, the quiet room.
But they never saw the warfare,
That started once and never left...

I screamed beneath a calm face,
fighting a battle with a ghost.
When even eating is a daily compromise,
How can I ever expect to live freely?

The empty desk in the corner,
Has stopped asking when I'd return.
The friends I used to walk with,
Are becoming strangers I once knew.

I used to be an achiever, a boy with maps,
Chasing a future carved out of expectations.
Now, the dreams have shrunk into a desperate prayer
Just let me get through today, just let me pass.

It didn't break in a single night.
It slipped; comparison first,
then expectation turned to pressure,
then reflection turned to fear,
until I was a living corpse
wearing everyone else's hope.

Slowly, the prayers were replaced by prescriptions.
The only future I had left,
Was packaged inside a handful of capsules,
That I had to swallow just to breathe.

Unrest

On the surface, I look elated,
But somewhere beneath the surface,
A restless current refuses to settle.

It carries no advance warning,
Ambushing mid-laughter,
In the middle of a melody;
In a space that finally felt completely safe.

A sudden tremor takes over me,
not fear exactly, just my body
speaking a language,
I never agreed to learn.

A pulse forgets its rhythm
Untethered from any cause.
And for a moment, I forget it too;
Who I was mid-sentence,
What I was about to say.

A heavy pressure gathers around my skull
like an inevitable storm, just running late.
While I have learned to smile through
this violent static serene on the surface.
While an unseen force,
Keeps testing the ground beneath me.

Some days, the flesh remembers,
What the mind cannot gather.
Some days, the mind clings
To what the body begs to forget.

And there is no symmetry to this chaos
And no hour it favors.
It simply breaks in, uninvited.
Inhabits whatever room it finds unlocked,
And vanishes just as silently as it came,

Leaving me to notice, hours later,
That something in me
Has shifted again

To the Medicine That Couldn't Heal Everything

Every cure asks for something in return,
Mine came in small, sterile payments,
Swallowed each day with water.

They promised to hold the broken pieces steady,
Just enough to let me stand,
Just enough to hold back the ghost.

But science has its limits.
It can slow a racing heart,
But not the fear that set it running.

It can quiet the screaming nerves,
But in return, it fogs everything else too...
The ambition, the pages I meant to finish,
The future I was halfway through building.

I stood on two feet, yes,
But I was walking through a stranger's life.

Wrapped in a heavy, sedated winter,
That I never asked for.

They were only temporary patches.
No chemical on earth can hand me back my lost years.

No prescription can rewrite the hundreds of sleepless nights,
or erase the terror that still lingers when the room goes dark....

To Death, When You Stood at My Door

I used to think of you as a distant shadow,

But when the suffering grew too heavy to carry,
I left the door unlocked for you,

Not out of carelessness,

But as an invitation.

I had already lost the argument with my own body.
It stopped listening long before I did,
So when you finally showed up,
I didn't ask you to leave.

I thought, if my own body can betray me,
If my hands can shake without my permission,
Why would you be any different?
Why would you listen either?

I wasn't afraid of you.
I was tired of everything,
That came before you.
So I tried reaching for your hand.

You felt less like an ending,
And more like a pause button,
That I wanted to press.
So I dropped all my weapons...

You didn't argue.
You just stepped back from the door,
The way an early guest
Quietly excuses themselves.

I don't know if you'll come back.
I only know that this time,
I opened the door on my own terms.
And you're the one,
Who left first....

The Room That Remembers

There is a scar hidden somewhere inside me,
And this room always knows where to look.

The moment I step in,
The white paint blinds me,
And the fluorescent light pulling me backward
Before I even chose to remember.

Then comes that suffocating
Distinct smell, clinical scent,
Only hospitals seem to carry.
It doesn't just fill the air.
It unearths my past.
Which I thought I'd sealed shut.

Suddenly I'm not here anymore.
I'm back in every hospital bed I've known,
back in rooms I can't name out loud,
back in the quiet terror
I thought I'd already survived.

Above me, the hum changes shape,
into something I recognize too well.
It becomes a steady, rhythmic beep of the OT monitors.

The kind that used to mean
someone was watching to see
if I'd make it through the night.

My chest tightens.
My heart forgets how to be calm.
My body remembers
Before my mind agrees to.

Sitting beneath these clinical, unblinking lights,
The hardest part isn't the memory itself.
It's the cruel realization settling into my bones.

That I thought I was getting better.

Quiet Hours

The world dies down by midnight,
But the noise inside my head starts waking up.
I lie awake, tossing and turning,
Anxious thoughts running endlessly through my mind.

Everything left pending,
the unread pages, the half-finished notes,
And the mountain of goals.
Yet I refused to let go..

Deep down, a stubborn spark tells me
I will achieve them.

While right beside it,
A loud, cold fear screams that
I won't make it through.

When the chaos gets too loud to bear,
When the ceiling feels like it's collapsing,
And across the room, my silent roommates:
The rows of medications, just stare back in the dark...

I finally give up.
I turn away from the empty bed,
And walk over to look outside my window.
There, under the endless span of the midnight sky.
I just gaze at the stars.

Sometimes my mind runs wild with self-blame,
But most times, I just stare and stare,
Completely empty of thoughts.

I don't know what I am looking for up there,
Or what answer I expect the darkness to hold.

But out here, watching the quiet universe breathe,
It gives me a strange, numbing relief.
It is better than the violence of tossing and turning.
It is a brief truce with the storm inside my skull,
Just me, the cold glass,
And a sky that doesn't ask me to explain.

The Dark Room

The curtains stayed shut,
Blending day and night into a single shade.
The room was never empty,
But started slowly swallowing my own thoughts.

I grew afraid of my own mind,
Deep down grieving for a life thrown off course,
Blaming myself for everything that fractured,
As if this entire war was a sentence I deserved.

Days and days, I tried to focus.
Tried to hold onto the words on the page,
But my mind wouldn't stay still,
And even try leaving new wounds behind.

In the quiet corner, my table held the past:
Stacks of books and notes frozen in time,
A calendar hanging uselessly on the wall,
Reminding me that life is moving forward.

Now, I was not the only resident,
The second bed belonged to a different roommate now.
It was neatly laid out with medication bills,
Long prescriptions, and rows of silent capsules.

Outside, the world kept moving at its regular pace.
Cars passed, footsteps faded, life carried on.
I started feeling disconnected from them,
But it was slow and tragic...

Half-Alive

The calendar on the wall keeps losing its pages,
But inside this room, time doesn't move.
It just repeats.

I have become a machine with a heartbeat,
Running on a clinical schedule:
Wake up. Swallow the capsule.
Stare at the corner where the shadow stays.
Eat because the body demands fuel.
Swallow the next tablet.
Sleep.
Repeat.

Outside, the world moves in high velocity.
People I grew up with are crossing oceans,
Building futures, leaving tracks in the dust.
Even those who walked behind me have moved ahead.
But I am stuck in the throat of a long winter,
Watching the traffic of life pass me by.

They are changing zip codes,
And the only thing changing around me
Is the name of my roommates,
Stacked on the nightstand, in silver tinfoil strips.

I don't feel sad anymore.
I don't feel happy either.
The high dosage has carved out a hollow space inside my chest,
A perfect stillness where nothing hurts,
And nothing breathes.

I am not dead enough for a grave,
But I am not alive enough for the sun.
I am just a living corpse, trapped in a loop,
Waiting for the sedation to wear off,
Or for the machine to finally shut down.

To Loneliness

You didn't arrive with a loud bang;
You slithered quietly.
The day my classes turned into appointments,
My attendance became a guest appearance.

Before my friendships could even grow roots,
You gave them a reason to fade away.

To the world, I was still the loud one,
The talkative, extroverted guy who laughed at every joke,
The self-appointed therapist fixing everyone else's broken pieces.

I treated my old friends like pearls,
Holding their heavy secrets safely in my hands,
While silently dropping my own into a bottomless dark.
I never learned how to say:
I am drowning too.

Surfacely, I was full of noise.
But inside, you were carving out a hollow machine.

Slowly, the drift became a landslide.
I vanished from social media grids,
I locked the door,
And I sat in the stagnation of my room.

My health became my shadow,
And my mind, an engine of non-stop overthinking.
I didn't want to burden anyone with my mess,
So I let you eat me alive,
piece by piece.

And with you came that nameless restlessness,
A violent, quiet vibration in my bones.
A panic that paralyzes, making it impossible to move,
Yet impossible to rest.

I searched for answers,
But the search only fed the fever.
Even when my eyes closed, sleep was just an empty room.

I woke up tired, heavy,
And unproductive.
Trapped in a skin that felt entirely too tight.

You aren't just the absence of people.
You are the presence of a heavy silence
That tells me I am completely on my own.

To Hope

You were the heaviest thing to carry in a room
That smelled like cold glass and bitter tablets.
I was terrified of you.
Because holding you meant expecting the sun,
And expectation was just a new kind of bleed.

I didn't find you in the loud places.
Not in the mornings that promised much and gave little,
Not in the mirrors that kept asking who I used to be.
I found you in smaller things.
The way you find a single window
Left open in a house you thought was sealed shut.

You were a cruel companion.
You forced a heart that wanted to sleep to keep beating.
You kept my eyes half-open during the longest sedation.
Making me watch the ceiling,
Whispering that the storm had an end.

You didn't arrive like a grand, exploding light
You arrived like breath.
Quiet, involuntary, the kind of thing a body does
Even when the mind has stopped asking it to.

You were the roommates on the table.
As the bitter tablets, the pills
That I absolutely hated,
Which somehow still meant I was choosing to stay.

And now, as the silver sheets of medicine strips lie empty,
And the room that remembers prepares to turn silent,
You aren't just a lingering shadow anymore.
You are a key turning into a long-locked door.
Forcing a heart that wanted to sleep to look toward the window.

Act III

The Second Sunrise

You don't go back to who you were; you meet the person who survived.

To Life, For Giving Me Another Chance

I am not shouting from the top of the Himalayas yet,
But for the first time in years,
My chest doesn't feel like a folding cage.

I owe a quiet thank you to Death
For standing at my door, looking inside,
And deciding to leave me alone that day.
When it walked away, it left heavy.

But now, relief felt strange on a body
That had forgotten what it was for.

It didn't happen in an instant.
But the air started tasting different.
The windows stopped looking like borders,
And the sky began to lose its clinical grey.

Nature had been there the whole time.
I was the one who'd been somewhere else.

After months of betrayal, of mechanical friction.
I finally felt a slow coordination returning.
A soft alignment in my limbs.
Sweet realization that my body is finally listening to me again.

I don't know when I stopped being a patient,
But I only know I looked up one morning,
And the sun was just the sun,
Not a countdown or a reminder

I am still fragile.
And getting better in a thousand small ones.
Each one quietly proving that I am allowed to stay.

The Person I Never Became

Sometimes, I see him in the quiet spaces.
The boy who was supposed to be holding a stethoscope right now.
If life had moved along its ordinary geometry,
Without any emergency stops, without any interruptions,
He would be walking down white hospital corridors
Not as a patient, but as a healer.

But life doesn't take requests.

An unchosen war rewrote the whole map,
Turned every straight road
Into something with more twists and turns
Than I ever planned for.

I look at his ghost, and I realize
That I can never be like before again.
I have seen the battlefield.
I have tasted the iron of survival.
You cannot unsee the dark nights where the sparks almost died.

But as I step out of that room.
I am not empty-handed.
The ocean shook me, but it also taught me things
That people need entire lifetimes to learn.
So let that ordinary boy rest in the pages that were never written.

I won't hold the stethoscope in the way I once imagined.
But my hands will build something somewhere else.
I am learning to hold myself in a way that version of me never had to.
I am not who I was supposed to become.
I am something rawer, still breathing, still standing,
Something that had to be forged instead of simply grown...

And maybe that's
Its own kind of becoming
Not the dream I lost,
But only this exact war could have built....

To My Reflection

I stood under the sharp white light.
Leaning against the cold edge of the sink.
And looked into the mirror
Expecting a familiar face.
But a stranger looked back instead.

I didn't recognize him right away.
The boy who entered that room months ago,
Had fuller cheeks, carefree plans, and an unfiltered laugh.
This reflection looked older, scarred, and tired.
Carrying the quiet dust of an unchosen war.

The skin had gone too pale,
Carrying the trace of clinical walls.
Eyes were sunken with dark hollows,
Holding all those sleepless nights.

But as I looked deeper into those eyes.
No anger, No disgust.
Just a wave of connection
As it told the story of the war I survived.

I felt completely transformed.
I washed the cold water over my face,
Watching the past swirl away,
knowing I might never get that old face back...

To My Scars

You are the lines I never wanted.
The sudden, uneven maps left on my skin
Of a territory that almost collapsed.
Before you came, my body was an unwritten page.
It was smooth, quiet, and carried no history.
Now, you are the permanent ink of a silent knife.
The rigid remnants of a long survival.

At first, I hated you.
I wanted to turn off the lights,
To hide the uneven geometry that split my flesh.
You looked like permanent defects,
Reminders of a body that had betrayed me,
Stamps of weakness that I never wanted the world to see.

But time moved, and the texture changed.
The redness faded into dark
And I realized that,
You are no longer open wounds.
But are closed gates.

You are the living proof that in the dark,
When my mind had given up,
My body was still quietly fighting for me.
The skin learned how to sew itself back together
While I was too weak to even look.

I trace you with my fingertips now.
Not with shame, but with huge respect and pride.
You are the medals I brought back
From a war I never chose.
Reminders that when my life was pushed to its absolute edge.

I broke, yes. But I survived.

To Time

You found me first as a boy.
I used to treat you like a background shadow.
You were just the ordinary ticking of a clock,
Which brought sunrises and sunsets.
I thought you were endless.
So, I spent you like someone who had too much to waste.

Then, the unchosen war broke out,
And you became my heaviest cage.
In those sterile hospital rooms,
you didn't move; you crawled.

I started counting you in appointments,
In sleepless nights.
In everything I was losing faster
Then I could hold onto it.

I hated you then.
When a single afternoon used to feel like an eternity of heavy noise.

You felt less like time passing and more like time taking:
my studies, my dreams
the boy I used to recognize
in the mirror.

But somewhere in the slowest stretch of you.
I started changing too but am still not fully healed.
The rage is fading, but the transition is slow.
Learning to walk with you, not away.

It is terrifying how much power you carry.
Within the gap of just a few short years.
You completely rewrote my timeline,
And taught me the actual value of a quiet breath.

You are the silent distance between
who I was and who I survived to be....

To Tomorrow

I stepped into you carrying what I learned from time.
I learned that maps could bleed,
That roads could snap without a warning,
And that survival isn't a loud celebration.
It is just a quiet breath you finally get to keep.

Time forced me to look at the cracks,
Acknowledging exactly how fragile we really are.
We are built of glass and sudden storms,
So easily shattered by an unchosen war,
So vulnerable in the dark when the floor falls away.

But I learned how to grow past fragility.
Not by pretending the wounds never happened,
But by letting the broken pieces fuse back together.
I learned that being fragile doesn't mean you stay broken;
It just means you have to build a stronger foundation.

So I chose to move forward with time,
Refusing to stay stuck on the hospital ceiling or the stolen years.
I loosen my grip on the ghost of who I used to be.
Stepping into coming hours with a steady foot,
Making sure I was a clean slate, not a repeated trap.

I don't know what you look like, Tomorrow.
You are still an unwritten page, a distant sunrise.
But I am walking toward you, unhealed but ready.
Breathing in the quiet air,
And finally, looking forward.

Act IV

Learning to Live

I survived the fire; now I refuse to sit in the dark.

To Fear

You used to be so loud.
You were the screaming sirens of the emergency room.
The flashing monitors and sudden panic in the night,
When my body felt like a building on fire.

I knew your face back then.
It was terrifying, but it was out in the open.
But now that the sirens have stopped,
You have changed your shape.

You have now become a quiet, lingering shadow,
Creeping into the silent corners of my recovery.
You hide in a sudden, random ache,
In the cold sweat before a routine checkup.

You whisper that the unchosen war isn't over;
That it's just waiting for the right moment to strike.
You try to make me a prisoner in my own skin,
Staring at hospital ceilings under an open sky.

You want me to believe my body is a betrayal waiting to happen.
But listen to me closely:
I am not the same boy you found on that ordinary morning.
I have looked into the eyes of death that even death refused to take.

I have traced the dark geometry of my scars,
And I have learned how to hold my breath in the dark.
You can sit in the shadow if you must,
A quiet reminder of how fragile I am.

But you no longer get to hold the wheel.
You are just a remnant of a war I survived,
And I am moving forward.
Even if my hands are still shaking.

To Peace

I find you here, after the long, unchosen war.
Yet somewhere in the endless night,
A tiny spark refused to die. It never shouted, never roared,
It softly whispered, "One step more."

So step by step, and scar by scar,
I traveled farther than I thought.
Until one day the chains gave way,
And darkness slowly lost its sway.

The air felt different on my face.
The sun returned with quiet grace.
Not because life had become fair,
But because I was still standing there.

Now every sunrise means far more
Than it had ever done before.
The ordinary feels divine,
A borrowed miracle called time.

I've learned that storms are never guests
Who promise they will always rest..
They come, they shake, they leave one day.
As every season finds its way.

So now I choose a gentler mind
To think with wisdom, not confined.
For stress builds prisons we can't see.
While thoughtful hearts walk quietly free.

Life will wound, and life will heal.
It turns its pages; that's its deal.
The waves will rise; the waves will cease.
Don't fight the ocean; sail in peace.

I was not broken by the fight.
I became someone who found the light.
And now I live with grateful eyes
For life itself... is the greatest prize.

To the Life I Finally Learned to Love

I once welcomed death with open hands,
Ready to surrender to the quiet and the cold.
But you refused to let my story end.
You sent me back into the world, bruised but alive.

I stopped mourning the paths I was meant to walk,
The ordinary dreams of doctors and engineers.
You tore down the future I thought I wanted,
To show me a purpose born from survive and endure.

We enter this world without a choice,
And we leave it when our time is called.
So why spend another hour rotting in depressed corners,
When today could be the last sunrise I ever see?

I forgive you now, life and I forgive time itself.
You gave me scars no book or classroom could teach.
You were never trying to break my spirit;
You were forging a warrior out of raw pain.

I don't fear what tomorrow holds anymore,
Because every storm behind me was just your brutal lesson.
I hold onto you now with both hands.
Grateful for this breath, this fight, and this second chance.

To My Final Breath

I remember when you were small and terrified,
Gasping for air on a stretcher, knowing nothing of pain.
An innocent boy caught in a war he never requested,
Wondering if each shallow gasp would be his last.

So many could not make it through the fire,
Broken by the weight, silenced by the storm.
But you held on through the monitors and scars,
Refusing to give up until the darkness gave way.

You did not just survive; you grew wise.
You learned the rules of life, stripped fear of its power,
And turned every brutal lesson into quiet strength,
No longer running from the shadows of tomorrow.

I leave nothing behind in the dark today,
For every tear and victory is written in this book.
My story is bled onto these pages in full,
A testament that pain is never the final word.

So when you finally leave my chest for good,
Do not carry my name; carry my message:
To anyone fighting in the quiet, unchosen dark
If a scared boy could survive the fire and win, so can you.

Epilogue

To the Reader

If you are reading these words, it means you have walked through the storm with me. You sat with the boy who thought life was simple, stood beside him in the ICU, witnessed his world break apart, and watched him learn how to rebuild out of the ashes.

I did not write this book to demand sympathy, nor did I write it to celebrate surviving a tragedy. I wrote it because for a very long time, I carried an unspoken war inside my chest—a war of fear, loneliness, anger, and gasping for air in the middle of quiet nights. For years, I didn't have the words or the courage to speak those feelings out loud. These poems were the conversations I never had.

If you are currently fighting your own unchosen war—whether inside a hospital room, behind closed doors, or beneath a smile nobody sees—I want you to remember this: You were born without a choice, and you will leave without one. But everything in between belongs entirely to you.

You do not have to spend your days rotting in depressed corners, waiting for the sky to fall. You do not have to remain terrified of the fire that burned you. The pain you survived was not meant to destroy your future; it was preparing you for tomorrow. It stripped away your fears, broke your expectations, and built a warrior where an innocent child once stood.

Put down your armor now. Take a deep breath—one that is steady, quiet, and fully yours.

Your story is not over. Step out of the dark, and go live the life you were meant to love.

— Aadarsh Niraula
